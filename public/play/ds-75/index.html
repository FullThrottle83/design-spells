The hour the harbour changes colour.

By Mara Ellingsen · 6 min read · Tromsø, September

There is a twenty-minute window, just after the sun touches the breakwater, when the whole harbour seems to hold its breath. The water goes the colour of weak tea; the gulls go quiet.

I have drawn this view eleven times now, and every version disagrees about the orange. The boost of colour lives mostly in the reflection path — a wavering column that the smallest ripple can erase.

Draw the water first. The boats will forgive you; the light will not wait.

The enlarged view shows the same drawing larger: count the shimmer bars in the sun path, then the gulls. Six strokes, three birds, one patient lighthouse.
